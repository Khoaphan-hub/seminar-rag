[Bùi Trọng Thành · 75 seconds] Because our questions share many exact terms with the paper, we asked what happens when the wording changes but the meaning does not. We prepared one paraphrase for each of the twenty questions and kept the answers, gold spans, corpus and settings the same. For example, “minimum Python and R versions” became “oldest supported releases of Python and R.” This was retrieval only, with no new Gemini calls. Note the metric: this table uses evidence recall, not the chunk recall from the previous chart. TF-IDF depends on overlapping words and phrases, and it fell from 80 percent to 55. MiniLM can represent semantic similarity, and it fell from 62.5 to 55. So MiniLM declined less, but both methods ended at the same evidence recall. This shows sensitivity to wording. It does not prove that embeddings are always better, and it says nothing about answer quality.
